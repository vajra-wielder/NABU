About NABU

Nabu was the Babylonian god of scribes, writing and wisdom. His symbol was a single wedge pressed into clay, the stylus that wrote the world's first libraries. His temples kept tablets and his priests copied them, so that knowledge outlived the people who wrote it down.

This NABU does the same for Wikipedia. Carry it on a pen drive, plug it into almost any computer, and the whole encyclopedia opens in the browser: every article, link, picture and table, without an internet connection.

How it works

NABU is a small program (about 10 MB) with versions for Windows, macOS and Linux on Intel, AMD and ARM processors. It reads Wikipedia from .zim files in the library folder and shows the pages to your browser at a private address on this computer. Nothing is installed and nothing is written to the computer you plug into.

When it starts and the computer is online, NABU asks the Kiwix catalogue whether a newer Wikipedia snapshot exists, and offers to download it onto the drive.
